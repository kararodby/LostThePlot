Breaking the Cycle: Changing How We Develop Technology to Have More Intentionality
Sunday, September 13 · 12:15–1:00 PM  ·  In-Person (Chicago)
Co-Sponsor: The Token Institute
Register to attend →
While technology is often the focus or catalyst of societal change, it is hardly ever developed with intentionality towards end use. Rather, technology has been treated as acontextual: it is developed and then applied to many different use cases that can have vastly different societal implications. It is also usually designed to solve one problem, while ignoring the other problems it may create. Industry-level regulation is often minimal, and we continue to see entire new sectors emerge for technologies that actively make peoples' lives worse.

These trends have only heightened over the last 50+ years with the rise of neoliberal politics. Since AI is a virtually brand new industry with the potential to significantly change society for better or worse, now is the time to break the cycle by slowing down and designing guardrails that limit unintended consequences, externalities, and direct harm, instead centering societal wellbeing. This is particularly important for mitigating the climate implications of the AI industry, which have the potential to accelerate climate change significantly in the coming decades.

This talk is a part of the 2026 Token Institute Film Conference, a cultural conversation built around collections of short films created with AI tools interspersed with discussions on the ethical evolution of this industry.

The Patience Deficit: Rethinking Capital for Climate Tech
Wednesday, September 9 · 5:30–7:30 PM  ·  In-Person (Chicago)
Co-Sponsor: Climate Action Museum
Event page →
Lost The Plot (LTP) co-hosted with Chicago's Climate Action Museum (CAM) for a Chicago Venture Week event. The event consisted of a presentation from LTP's Kara Rodby on the shortcomings of the venture capital model for scaling important climate tech, followed by a fireside chat where Kara was joined by Michelle Carr of Evergreen Climate Innovations and Matt Orsagh of the Arketa Institute to discuss how these folks think about changing the system. 15 people attended and gave strongly positive feedback that they appreciated the content and conversation, which was repeatedly noted as unique compared to other conference/seminar/panel offerings. This event may lead to more partnerships between LTP and CAM, stay tuned.
Chicago Climate Week · July 2026
Losing the Growth, Finding the Plot: How the Concept of a Circular Economy Can Be Fortified by the Challenge to Growth
Thursday, July 23 · 5:30–7:00 PM  ·  In-Person (Chicago)
Co-Sponsor: Degrowth Institute
Event page →
Lost The Plot co-hosted with Chicago's own Degrowth Institute (DGI) for a Chicago Climate Week event. With presentations from DGI's Jason Barahona Rosales and circular economy specialist Madeleine Sullivan, we talked about the fundamentals of degrowth and then applied the theory to the concept of the circular economy. This exercise helped exemplify how well-meaning sustainability initiatives may still miss the point — a key theme of Lost The Plot — and in this case can be fortified by the challenge to growth. With over 40 attendees, including a good handful that hadn't ever interacted with degrowth concepts before, we achieved our goal of engaging people in honest and unique conversations about real climate action.
